## 3 · P0 — THE PRODUCT PANEL DOES NOT MOVE

It was rebuilt with `innerHTML` on every paint, so every value that changed length changed
the layout: `03 · 01` → `03 · 11` moved the price, `Standard` → `Premium` moved the button,
`$549,000` → `$699,000` re-centred the row. It has fixed geometry now — a stated width, a
stated row for every fact, tabular numerals, one slot per value — and a change of suite
writes TEXT into slots that do not move. Nothing is rebuilt; one button does both jobs
rather than two buttons being swapped in and out of the markup.

`dock.mjs`, ten changes including 03 → 04 → 06, suite 01 → 02 → last, and both
Premium ↔ Standard and $549,000 ↔ $699,000, measured on the **published** full site:


## 4 · CAMERA — THE 1/10

**It was not a short transition. It was not moving at all.**

`flyTo` tweened with the GSAP instance a host supplies through `api.useGsap()`. `main.js`
does that for production act 02; the V6 shell and `v6-boot.js` never did. So in every V6
context that reference was null, `flyTo` took its instant branch, and the camera was
*assigned* its new pose on the same frame the state changed. Every selection in the
published build was a cut, in the standalone page and in the integrated page alike.

The interpolator lives in the model now and runs off the render loop, so nothing about the
camera waits on a host library. Shortest-way azimuth; an arc that lifts and stands off
through the middle of a move and settles back in; duration from the real travel distance;
quintic in and out, so there is no snap and no lookAt pop at either end.

Two further faults had to be fixed before it read as travel:

- **The tween accumulated the frame-clamped `dt`**, which ties a duration to the frame
  rate. The same 1.1 s move measured **3.3 seconds** under load, creeping for two of them
  and then rushing. It runs on the wall clock now.
- **Switching the nine practical lights on changed the scene's point-light count**, and
  Three bakes that count into every compiled program. The first selection of a session
  blocked the main thread for about 1.2 s recompiling, and because the tween runs on real
  time it arrived already finished. The one remaining cut was the *first one a visitor
  ever makes*. The lights are permanent at zero intensity, so the count never changes.

`travel.mjs`, sampling the camera's world position every 40 ms:

## 5 · MATERIALS — THE 1/10

Every surface was one colour and one roughness, so a 202-foot precast wall and a 4-foot
door panel returned the same substance at two sizes.

Detail is now computed from **world position in feet**, injected into the standard
material's own shader: precast panel joints at a real casting size, sectional door panels
with the ribs inside them, saw-cut apron joints, membrane laps, asphalt grain, coping
seams. One material per surface, no downloads, and the panel size is correct on a 202-foot
run and a 23-foot bay alike because both read the same world coordinate — which also means
the joints line up across two boxes that meet.

An image map was ruled out for three reasons, all of them practical: a box's UVs run 0..1
per face so a shared texture is a different panel size on every mesh; the published folder
already carries 9.8 MB of geometry; and a photographed concrete map brings someone else's
building's stains to a new-construction site at dusk.

Two things were found by looking rather than by reasoning:

- **Crawling dot moiré** in the specular on the roofs — procedural lines drawn at a fixed
  width in feet fall below the sampling rate at a grazing angle. Every line is now
  `fwidth`-aware and fades out once it is smaller than a pixel.
- **A speckled ring on the roundabout** that read as gravel or standing water. Two causes:
  fine grain that should have disappeared at compound distance (it now does), and an
  asphalt roughness swung by a third of its value, which took the drive down to 0.52 in
  patches — smooth enough to return the sky. Floored at 0.58.

**The walls are sand precast**, not blue-grey metal at metalness 0.34. That is what the
reference is, and it is also what puts act 02 back in the same evening as act 00.

## 7 · LIGHTING — THE 2/10, AND THE FLOATING POOLS

**The rule: every visible light has a source, a direction, and a surface it lands on.**

The cool pool that kept appearing on the roof of the building *in front of* whichever
building was selected was traced — by raycasting the pixels it occupied — to the focus
fill: a point light hanging in the air 38 feet off the subject's roof, 30 feet up, with a
hundred-foot reach, and no object anywhere near it. **Deleted, and nothing replaces it.**

In its place, the architecture has lights. Every bay carries a real hooded downlight — a
cast body on a bracket with a lit lens underneath it — and nine point lights are re-hung on
the fittings of whichever run is the subject, just below the lens and slightly proud of the
wall, so the wash runs down the elevation, across the leaf and out onto the apron. A
hundred and sixteen lights would be a different kind of project; nine that move to where
they are needed cost one shadowless light per fitting on one building at a time.

They come up **in sequence along the run**, which is also the building hover: warm light
travelling along the architectural rhythm rather than a fill changing value. Staggered in
the render loop, so it works with no host library and honours reduced motion.

The parapet coping was also catching the key as a smeared pool along a 263-foot bar; a
coated coping is not a mirror, and at 0.88/0.04 it now catches it as a line along its edge.

## 8 · THE FACADE — THE 2/10

- **The glazed head was 0.6 ft BEHIND the leaf.** The leaf sits at faceD + 0.04 and is 0.5 ft
  deep; the glass was at faceD − 0.30, *inside the suite*. No review has ever seen a door
  with a window in it. It is in the leaf's plane now, set back inside the reveal where a
  glazed top section belongs, with a meeting rail and two glazing bars.
- **The sectional joint was a raised bar.** Three trim boxes per leaf, standing proud —
  363 meshes drawing the joint between two door panels as the opposite of what it is. The
  door recipe cuts it as a recess, at a real 20-inch panel pitch, with the ribs inside each
  panel, and costs no meshes.
- **The wall fixture was a bright quad.** It is a hood, a bracket and a lens now, and the
  light that appears on the wall comes from a point just under it.
- Piers, reveals, the red corner reveal from the elevation, the concrete apron and the
  parapet assembly were already built and are unchanged.
- **The chosen suite stops glowing.** A whole leaf with a warm emissive is a lamp shaped
  like a door, and at the ENTER pose it came back cream-white with its panels washed out.
  The glazed head goes warm — light behind the window — and the leaf stays dark coated
  steel.
- **Exposure stops down as the camera closes on a facade**, the way an eye does. It only
  bites inside four world units, so every composed frame above that is untouched.

## 9 · THE BLACK SLAB — REMOVED, AND HERE IS WHAT IT WAS

Traced by raycasting the pixels it occupied: a **0.3 ft × 11.4 ft × 67 ft plate in open air**
off the west end of the clubhouse. It was the civic glazed band's frame, and the cause was
one transposed rotation. Three's `rotation.y = t` maps a local point to
`(x cos t + z sin t, y, −x sin t + z cos t)`; this block computed its offset as
`(x cos t − z sin t, y, x sin t + z cos t)` — the rotation by *minus* t. The mesh was
oriented one way and pushed the other, and on a face of a rotated building the two disagree
by twice the angle. The clubhouse's second wing sits at −29.3°, which is why that one threw
a plate into the sky while the 6.5° wing beside it looked almost right. `roofAssembly`
already had the convention correct; the mullions and rails had the same fault and were
fixed with it.

Inspected at overview, building 01, 03 and 10, and at the low facade view
(`exploration/audit/slabs/`): no unexplained slab.

## 10 · SUITE HOVER

The apron pool is now a request the frame loop rolls in over about a fifth of a second
rather than an intensity assigned in one step — the difference between light arriving on a
door and a rectangle changing colour. The tag rises the last few pixels into place instead
of blinking on; its position still comes from the model every frame, so it never lags the
door it names. The door's own material, its glazed head and its wall fitting all still
change together, and hovering a miniature bay still lights the real door — measured:
available bay → apron 0.82, sold bay → 0, chosen → 1.15.

## 12 · BUILDINGS 02 AND 10 — THE TWO ROWS

The run grouping was keyed on the run's **angle**. Both rows of building 02 sit at −22.98°
and their doors face opposite ways, so the two rows landed in one group whose mean normal
is zero — the model had been reporting one elevation where there are two. Keyed on the
direction the doors face, building 02 reports 2.

- The building carries a **list** of its elevations, best first, and the camera arrives at
  the current one.
- The building tag says **"15 of 26 available · two rows"** before you go in, which is the
  only place that fact can be learnt: once you are inside, the second row is behind the
  first.
- **One control, only where the architecture has two rows** — nine of the eleven never show
  it. It flies the camera round the *end* of the building to the other elevation: half a
  turn has two equal ways round and the shorter-arc rule cannot choose, so the move names
  the way that passes the end rather than sweeping through the mass.

Building **10** turns out not to be a two-row building in this sense: all sixteen of its
doors face the same way, in two runs one behind the other, and a single camera shows both.
That is a correction to the brief's premise, measured rather than assumed.

## 14 · REMAINING VISUAL FAILURES

1. **Building 02's second elevation is a weaker composition than its first.** The swing is
   applied with the same sign on both sides, so the run recedes the mirrored way and fewer
   of its fittings are in frame. It works; it is not yet as well composed as the first.
2. **The ENTER pose is still bright.** Stopping the exposure down fixed the clipping and
   the door's panels read, but the precast around a lit bay is close to the top of the
   range at three feet. A real facade would fall off faster than a point light does.
3. **The building pose is composed for a 3:2 desktop stage only.** No V6.1 measurement has
   been taken at a phone's square stage, and the arrival framing has not been re-composed
   for it.
4. **The compound rest pose still has a large quiet foreground.** It reads as site now
   rather than as void, but the bottom third of the frame carries little.
5. **The trees are the one asset that has not had this pass.** They are the only imported
   geometry in the model and they now sit beside surfaces with more material than they have.
6. **The 02 → 03 aperture is still designed and parked.** The data survives the cut; the
   transition does not exist. `BRIEF-seam-02-03.md` carries the concept.
7. **The dashboard repository is still unpushed.** This repository is live; the GD Buro
   dashboard entry that points at it is a separate repo and is still waiting on a decision.

## 2. Dialect selection, in full

### Why not the obvious ones

- **`immersive-authored-world`** would be the reflex for "spatial experience". It is
  `confirmed` and it is **never inferred** — selected only when Alex names it, and
  never recommended because a brief sounds creative (`TASTE.md §4`). Not selected.
- **`technical-luxury`** would be the natural Contrast; its own file names
  `+ cinematic-industrial` as the pairing it is strongest in. It is **provisional**
  and usable only when Alex selects it explicitly (`dialects/README.md`). Not
  selected. `swiss-editorial` takes the same responsibilities — information as
  designed objects, the declared index — from `library` status, which an agent may
  select on the brief's merits.
- **`auction-editorial`** is not the silent fallback. An absent brief is not a
  compatible brief, and this one is present: it asks for weight, physical space and
  a walked sequence, not for air-first restraint and a lot record.

### The pair, derived (`HYBRID.md`, the six-step derivation — this pairing is not in
the compatibility table)

1. **Theses.** cinematic-industrial: *light, scale and material consequence do the
   persuading.* swiss-editorial: *a declared system carries the meaning; position and
   interval do the explaining.*
2. **The axis they disagree on:** atmosphere vs. order. One wants you immersed and
   slightly lost; the other wants you oriented at all times.
3. **The reconciling sentence:** *the plan tells you where you are; the light tells
   you what it is like to be there.* A private compound is exactly a place where both
   are true — you are inside something surveyed, numbered and gated, and it is dark
   and quiet and yours.
4. **Split by domain** — the CONTROL MAP below.
5. **Where the Contrast stops:** swiss-editorial owns the index, the plan, captions,
   specification and numbering. It never touches the photographic field, the light,
   the crop or the depth. There is no white editorial section.
6. **The collapse:** two dark restrained systems merging into one mood with no facts
   in it — `cinematic-industrial`'s own second false version, *atmosphere instead of
   evidence*. Named in COLLISION RISKS and checked by the record column existing in
   every act.

**The incompatibility test, answered** — 1 atmosphere vs. order · 2 because a private
compound is both immersive and administered, and selling one without the other is
either a mood film or a floor plan · 3 the unifying principle above · 4 the CONTROL
MAP · 5 at the edge of the photographic field · 6 a white editorial section, or a
plan that measures nothing · 7 remove swiss-editorial and the page becomes an
atmosphere with a phone number; remove cinematic-industrial and it becomes a real
estate brochure. Neither is removable, so this is a genuine HYBRID rather than a PURE
that has not admitted it.

## 3. The central metaphor

> **The user is not browsing a website about a compound. They are being admitted
> through one.**

Three claims sit under that, and each one produces a decision rather than a mood:

**A compound is a sequence of enclosures, not a space you fly through.** Privacy is
the experience of doors closing behind you. So the page moves by **cut**, not by
continuous travel: each chapter is a room you are now in, and the seam between two
chapters is an aperture — a door, a gate, a shutter — passing across the frame. The
previous build in this folder (`_buro test 1`) was a *continuous world*: one ground,
one light, one camera, no cuts. This is deliberately the opposite grammar (`DNA36`).

**A paddock is a gated place where machines are prepared and the people who own them
stand around.** That is why chapter 05 is not a hotel lobby: the club exists because
the machines are next door, and the page has to make the adjacency legible. The
paddock reading is used as *logic*, never as costume — no pit-lane graphics, no
timing-screen typography, no chequered anything (`retro-futurist`'s failure mode).

**The product is space, and the car is the evidence that the space matters.** This is
the load-bearing correction to the brief's reference. Luxe Corsa's current site sells
an amenity list with cars photographed near it; a generic luxury-real-estate page
sells a building. Here the enclosure is the dominant subject and the car is what
proves its proportions. It is why the photography is architecture-with-a-car-in-it
and never a three-quarter hero car with a building behind it (`DNA31`, `C13`).

## 5. The feeling curve (`DNA29`)

The emotion first, the thing on screen that causes it second. No two adjacent acts
share a feeling.

| # | Act | Feeling | Caused by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | ENTRY | **Exclusion, then invitation** | The perimeter from outside, at night. One aperture lit. The page states plainly what it is and where. |
| 01 | ARRIVAL | **Scale — this is not a garage** | The frontage passes laterally. Doors, doors, doors, at a length the frame cannot end. |
| 02 | THE COMPOUND — 121 PRIVATE SUITES | **Recognition, then specificity** | The light goes flat and the thin drawing that has walked beside them since act 01 opens out into the whole compound. *That line was this place.* Then: 121 suites, 11 buildings, one of them selectable. |
| 03 | THE SUITE | **Possession** | One door lifts. The room is empty, lit, and the proportions are yours. Then the car is in it. |
| 04 | MACHINE / SPACE | **Physical intimacy** | Macro: the door seal, the floor, the light crossing paint. No whole car, no whole room. |
| 05 | THE CLUB | **Company, after solitude** | The first frame in the page with people in it, and the first warm one. |
| 06 | COMMUNITY / OWNERSHIP | **Seriousness** | Flat ground again. What you actually own, on what terms, on what calendar. |
| 07 | PRIVATE TOUR | **Resolution — the door held open** | One frame, one line, one action. The camera stops. |

The two flat acts (02, 06) are the page's structural pauses and they are placed
either side of the peak's aftermath, so the rhythm is: enclosure · enclosure · flat ·
**peak** · enclosure · enclosure · flat · close.

## 6. The peak (`DNA28`)

> *"They put you inside one of the rooms and just let you stand there — and then the
> car appears and you realise how big it actually is."*

**Act 03 — THE SUITE.** It gets the longest extent on the page (3.4 of ≈13.8
viewport-heights), the only locked-camera multi-state transformation, the silence of
act 02's flat surveyed ground in front of it, and the entire photographic asset
budget's best frame.

The other seven acts are demoted relative to it on purpose. **Act 02 is the
mechanism; act 03 is the payoff.** The plan is what gets you here and it is
deliberately shorter, flatter and less lit than the room it opens onto — the map is
never the destination. **The dominant is the subject, not the device presenting it**
(`C13`), and a signature interaction that becomes the peak is the device taking the
subject's place.

## 7. The single signature interaction (`DNA37`)

> ### PLAN → SELECT → ENTER
>
> A thin architectural drawing accompanies the visitor from the first screen. It is
> never explained. At act 02 it **opens out into the compound**, gains shallow depth,
> becomes selectable, and one suite becomes the spatial subject — **and then that
> suite's own footprint becomes the frame the physical room appears through.** The
> drawing does not transition to a photograph; it *becomes* the opening the
> photograph is seen through, and then it is gone.

Three movements, and each is load-bearing:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **PLAN** | The rail expands into the compound | *Recognition.* The visitor understands what has been walking beside them. This is the payoff described in §15. |
| **SELECT** | One building, then one bay, becomes the subject | *Specificity.* 121 stops being a number and becomes an object with a size and a price. |
| **ENTER** | The selected footprint enlarges, becomes a mask, and the room appears through it | *Consequence.* The drawing is revealed to have been a description of a real volume all along. |

**Why it is a signature and not a device.** The test in `DNA37` is whether someone who
has seen the other builds could tell it apart. The previous build's device was a
hairline holding level across a machine's travel. This one is **a drawing that turns
out to be a place, and then turns into the doorway.** Its bespoke part is not the
expansion — plenty of sites expand a minimap — it is that **the same geometry does
three different jobs in sequence**: navigation, then selection, then aperture.

**Why it is not the peak.** It is the means of arriving; act 03 is the arrival.

**Why it is discoverable without instruction** (`DM7`, `MJ7`): the rail moves with the
scroll for a whole act before it is ever asked to be touched, so it explains itself by
behaving. It becomes interactive only where it is full-stage, labelled and obviously a
control. Nothing is gated behind understanding it.

### The PLAN RAIL, redefined

**The Plan Rail is the page's persistent architectural and navigational language. It
is not, by itself, the signature.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | One continuous architectural drawing, present from act 00 to act 07, whose state reports the visitor's position in the compound |
| **What it does** | Orients (band 2). Nothing else. It is the darkest, thinnest mass on screen and it never carries the optical centre |
| **What it becomes** | At act 02 only: the full stage, and the signature's first movement |
| **What it leaves behind** | A footprint notation in act 03's record, a lit clubhouse mass in act 05, a type diagram in act 06, and one lit bay in act 07 |
| **What it must never become** | A minimap, a progress bar with a percentage, a wayfinding widget, or a second navigation |

The distinction matters because it settles a real risk: a persistent element that is
*also* the signature tends to grow until it competes with the content it is supposed
to be locating. Demoting it to language, and promoting the three-movement transition
to signature, means the rail can stay quiet for six of eight acts and still pay off.

## 8. Page grammar (`DNA36`)

**Split stage — plan and room, cut-seamed.** A persistent narrow index at the left
edge (the plan) against a full-bleed stage that changes by cut (the room). Chapters
do not flow into each other; they are separated by an aperture crossing the frame.

**How it differs from the last build.** `_buro test 1` was a *continuous world* —
one ground, one light, one camera, no cuts, dimensionality MAIN, a single machine
subject, seven camera stations on one unbroken timeline. This build is split stage,
eight cuts, no continuous camera, dimensionality SUPPORT, and 3D confined to a single
act. The two builds share a dark ground and a mono record and nothing else
structural.

## 9. Composition Read (`§2a`)

1. **Artistic image** — a lit road between two rows of closed doors, and one of them
   open.
2. **Format forces** — the viewport's long axis is horizontal, the same proportion as
   a door opening and as a plan sheet; the scroll axis is vertical, which is walking.
   The format is used as a corridor.
3. **Major masses (5)** — (a) the photographic field, full-bleed; (b) the plan rail,
   a fixed hairline column at the left edge; (c) the record — spec plates and
   captions, a right-hand column group; (d) the identity plate — wordmark, location,
   act number, top edge; (e) the action mass, present as a persistent hairline
   affordance and full-size only at 07.
4. **Centres** — semantic: the suite. Optical: the lit aperture in each frame.
   Geometric: unused. Centre-of-action: the rail's marker. **The optical centre
   governs** — the eye lands on light in every frame, and the rail is deliberately
   never where the eye goes first.
5. **Dominance** — by luminance and scale before size. The lit mass wins every frame;
   the rail is the darkest, thinnest thing on screen.
6. **Balance** — optical and asymmetric throughout. Photographic mass right of centre,
   rail plus record counterweighting at left. No symmetric composition anywhere.
7. **Direction** — into the frame from the left, then down. Each act's implied
   direction points at the next act's aperture.
8. **Rhythm** — eight beats; the two flat acts are the departures, and they are the
   only two. Regular and slightly relentless in between, which is the mechanical
   rhythm the Anchor asks for.
9. **Negative space** — the unlit portion of each enclosure is active: it is the part
   of the building you are not being shown, and that *is* what privacy looks like.
   Not margin.
10. **Tension** — **121 against 1.** The index insists there are many; the peak
    insists only one is yours. That single opposition is the page.
11. **Edges / depth** — full bleed on the photographic masses, hairlines elsewhere.
    Depth from occlusion and light fall-off only; no elevation shadow anywhere
    (`U9`).
12. **Unity** — one light temperature across every frame, because the compound has
    one lighting design; one grotesque; one hairline weight; one crop discipline.
13. **Type as mass** — subordinate in six of eight acts. Its two moments of scale are
    the identity plate at 00 and the closing line at 07.
14. **Imagery forces** — wide, level, tripod-height, interior-architectural. The car
    is in the frame at the size a car is. No low hero angles, no rolling shots.
15. **Responsive** — the rail becomes a top-edge progress line; the plan becomes a
    numbered list; every full-bleed frame is re-cropped to portrait by hand.
16. **Functional** — the means of the task survive every composition: see a suite,
    read what it costs and what it includes, book a tour, call. These are reachable
    from any scroll position, not only from act 07 (`C14`).
17. **Diagnosis** — the failure this page is most likely to have is *atmosphere
    instead of evidence*. The counterweight is structural: the record column exists
    in all eight acts, and act 06 is a full act of nothing but terms.

## 10. Composition Plan

- **Mass scheme** — the five masses above, in that rank, unchanged across all eight
  acts. A new mass never appears mid-page.
- **Primary centre** — the lit aperture. Every act composes one.
- **Hierarchy mechanism** — luminance, then scale, then position. Never weight, never
  colour.
- **Eye path** — light → subject → record → rail → next aperture.
- **Rhythm** — enclosure · enclosure · flat · peak · enclosure · enclosure · flat ·
  close.
- **Negative space** — one large unlit region per frame, and it is composed, not left.
- **Tension** — 121 : 1, stated at 02 and resolved at 03.
- **Culmination** — act 03, stage three: the car in the room, at true proportion.
- **Cropping** — commit. Frames crop through the subject rather than around it; no
  tangency between a silhouette and a frame edge (`C9`).
- **Responsive recomposition** — re-establish the governing idea, not the content
  arrangement: on one column, the aperture cut and the numbered record carry the
  metaphor, and the rail's job moves to a position line.
- **Functional realisation** — a persistent hairline header carries wordmark ·
  location · phone · TOUR. It is counted as a mass (`U10`), it never goes opaque over
  live content (`U13`), and it is the reason no act needs to repeat a primary CTA.

**Not decided here, and deliberately.** The palette, the type scale, the grid's
column count and the spacing ramp are the next artefact. Naming them before the
composition would make them the thing the composition is arranged around.

## 11. Critique Panel — on the direction, before anything is built (`§2b`)

**1 · Composition critic.**
The single central idea is one sentence and it is legible: *121 closed doors, one
opened for you.* Devices that strengthen it: the aperture cut (it is the metaphor,
mechanised), the plan rail (it makes 121 a fact rather than an adjective), the
luminance hierarchy. Devices that compete: the rail is the risk — it sits at the edge
of every frame and edges are where the eye goes when a composition is not resolved.
Question 4: no section belongs to a different art direction *as declared*, but act 06
is the one where drift will happen, because the moment the terms need a table someone
will reach for a light ground. Question 5: no — one grotesque doing roles by size and
one mono for measurement is two voices for seven roles. Question 6: I would cut the
*third* stage of the peak before I would cut anything else, and I would keep it: what
is strengthened by removing "the car appears" is nothing, and what is lost is the only
proof of proportion on the page. The page is not over-devised. It is arguably
**under**-devised at act 04, which currently has one idea (macro) and 1.4 viewport
heights.

**2 · Craft critic.**
Nothing is measurable yet, which is the correct state at a concept gate, but three
numbers are already owed and are not in this document: the frame budget for the act-02
scene, the payload ceiling, and the LCP target. `DM3` requires them *before building*,
and they are specified in [motion-dna.md](motion-dna.md) — verify they are there
before markup. Two invariants are pre-loaded with risk: `DM5` (AA per frame over the
act-02 scene, which moves) and `color I4` (contrast measured on the composited render
for every caption sitting on photography). The 14px functional floor is going to fight
the mono record — mono runs optically smaller, and a spec plate at 12px is decoration.
The reduced-motion path is authored per act in the motion doc rather than promised
globally, which is right.

**3 · User advocate.**
Top three jobs: *is there a suite that fits my car and my budget* · *what do I
actually get for the money* · *can I see it*. Job one is served at 02 and nowhere
else, and it is served by a device that is only interactive during one act — if I
scroll past 02 and then want to change my suite, the page currently has no answer.
Fix that or the rail is a trap. Job two lands at 06, which is 11 viewport-heights in;
a buyer who arrived from a paid ad will not reach it. Job three is fine — the tour is
in the persistent header from the first screen. What will annoy on the fiftieth use is
the aperture cut: a device at every seam is a device you have watched fifty times, and
the person using this page fifty times is the sales team.

**4 · Brief advocate.**
The brief says *cinematic, architectural, automotive, spatial, premium, members-only,
heavy and physical, highly art-directed*, and the direction serves all eight —
"architectural" and "spatial" especially, because the correction that the enclosure is
the product is what makes them structural rather than decorative. The avoid-list is
answered structurally rather than by intention: no card grid (the record is a column,
not tiles), no generic fade-up (the entrance vocabulary is aperture and cut), no
particles (there is a rule banning them by name), no black-and-gold (the palette is
luminance-driven and unspecified). One drift risk to name: the brief asks for
*members-only*, and the strongest way to express that is a gate — which `MJ7` and
`DM2` forbid. The direction converts exclusivity into *what you are shown* rather than
*what you must do first*, and that is the right trade, but it is a trade and it should
be visible in the report.

**5 · Contrarian.** *(Mandatory dissent.)*
The strongest case against this direction: **it is a real-estate page wearing a film's
clothes, and the film is going to lose.** Somebody buying a $200k garage suite has
four questions — price, size, terms, availability — and this page answers the first
three at act 06 and the fourth through a device that only works once. Every one of the
eight acts is beautiful and only two of them are *load-bearing for the sale*. The
second objection is asset dependency, and it is severe: `cinematic-industrial` has
nothing to light if the photography is not excellent and consistent, and this compound
is a half-built development in Illinois — the imagery that exists is renders and
drone shots, not an authored interior lighting design. The vault records that exact
failure three separate times. My proposed alternative worth considering: **invert the
sequence.** Open at the plan — 121 numbered units, availability visible in the first
screen — and use the cinematic acts as the *reward* for having chosen, not as the
approach to choosing. That is a genuinely different page and it might sell more suites.

## 12. Judgment calls

- **REDESIGN over REBRAND**, with the visual identity recorded as an empty carrier.
  The alternative reading is defensible; it is flagged in §1 rather than asked as a
  question, and it is reversible.
- **`swiss-editorial` in the seat `technical-luxury` would naturally take.** The
  responsibilities are nearly identical for this brief; the difference is that one is
  selectable by an agent on the brief's merits and the other requires Alex. If Alex
  selects `technical-luxury`, the CONTROL MAP transfers almost unchanged and the spec
  plate replaces the declared index as the record's shape.
- **Dimensionality SUPPORT, not MAIN.** MAIN was available and would have matched the
  brief's ambition. It was rejected because the compound is photographable, and a
  page whose depth is MAIN cannot be honestly claimed to stand without it. The
  previous build was MAIN; two MAIN builds in a row is a house style forming.
- **The signature move is not the peak.** Deliberate, and the less impressive of the
  two available structures.

## 13. Vault reads — decisions taken, never artefacts copied

Each read states the **principle**, and the third clause is a different concrete move
from the reference's own. A read that produces *"the reference does X, so do X"* is a
failed read.

**`vault/rolls-roycemotorcars-com-en-us-home-html`** (3, in) solves *the interface must
not compete with the object* by making video, UI and background read as **one
continuous environment rather than layered rectangles** — which here, where the object
is a room and not a car, means the record column and the rail sit **on the
photograph's own ground and light** rather than on a panel over it, and the seam
between chapters is a door the building actually has rather than a rectangular wipe.
*Its recorded weakness is the instruction:* frosted-glass legibility varying with the
footage behind it. **So there is no translucent surface over imagery anywhere on this
page** — captions occupy the frame's quiet region and are measured on the composited

**`vault/semlerpremium-dk-showroom`** (3, in) solves *an inventory can be desirable

mood chapter about exclusivity. *Weakness to avoid:* minimal presentation withholding
what comparison shoppers need immediately, so **dimensions and status live on the plan

**`vault/semlerpremium-dk-brands-porsche-911-gt3-…`** (3, in) solves *one item in a

**`vault/porsche-com-usa`** (2, hybrid) solves *moving a visitor from wanting to

опасный референс»: full-screen car plus giant clean type plus black rounded cards

**`vault/oilstainlab-com`** (3, in) solves *a rare machine needs mythology before

## 14. Is Three.js justified for the Compound scene?

**No — and the reasoning matters more than the answer, because the answer has a
declared trigger that would reverse it.**

### What the concept actually requires

| Requirement | Can constructed 2D do it? |
|---|---|
| 121 bays, 11 buildings, a clubhouse, readable as one compound | **Yes.** This is a plan. Plans are drawings. |
| Shallow extrusion, fixed shallow axis, roads nearly planar | **Yes.** Pre-projected geometry on one fixed axonometric axis. |
| Hover → subtle interior illumination | **Yes.** A light-spill gradient on the floor plane plus an interior-face value lift. |
| Selection → the object feels physically activated | **Yes, and measurably.** The lit bay reads at **3.91:1** against the unlit mass and **4.45:1** against the ground — a real activation with no selection border at all (`visual-direction.md §3`). |
| Level 02 reframe, other buildings subordinate | **Yes.** A `viewBox` tween is a genuine camera, and subordination is by luminance rather than by an overlay. |
| Suite subdivisions revealed only when needed | **Yes**, trivially. |
| **The footprint becoming the frame the room appears through** | **Yes, and more easily than in 3D.** The footprint is already a path; the same path becomes a `clip-path`. From WebGL this requires projecting the geometry to screen space and handing off to the DOM anyway. |
| Keyboard access, real accessible names, focus order | **Yes — and a canvas cannot.** A `<canvas>` has no accessible children; the DOM layer would have to exist regardless, and then it is doing the work twice. |
| No-WebGL fallback, reduced-motion still, mobile | **It *is* the fallback.** |

### What WebGL would add, honestly

Three things, and only three: true parallax between masses during a reframe; real
light fall-off and contact shadow; and a changeable viewing axis.

**The brief rules out all three by its own description.** *"Restrained perspective ·
shallow building extrusion · roads remaining nearly planar · minimal surrounding
context · must not feel like an architectural CAD viewer."* A fixed shallow axis with
no free rotation **is a drawing**, and `DM6` says the view gets one depth idea — here
that idea is *extrusion*, not *camera*.

### The decisive argument

**The 2D version has to be built anyway.** It is the no-WebGL path, the reduced-motion
still, and the mobile execution. So the choice is not *2D or 3D* — it is *2D, or 2D
plus a renderer that duplicates it.* Build the drawing, look at it, and the question
answers itself with an artefact instead of an opinion.

### The declared upgrade trigger — the only one

> **If the compound act must deliver a *material* read rather than a drawn one** — roof
> surfaces, wet asphalt, true contact shadow between mass and ground, the compound as a
> photographed physical model rather than as a technical drawing — **then it is a scene
> and WebGL is justified.** Promotion at that point re-runs the Design Read, because it
> changes the dominant and the Anchor's relationship to the act.

Nothing in the current concept asks for that. The act is the page's one *surveyed*
register — the moment the visitor is told facts — and making it photographic would
collapse the distinction the whole page is built on.

### What this means for the stack

| | |
|---|---|
| **Homepage act 02** | SVG geometry + GSAP. No renderer. |
| **Homepage, everywhere else** | Photography, CSS, GSAP. No renderer. |
| **Three.js on the homepage** | **Not loaded at all.** Not lazily, not conditionally — it is not in the dependency graph. |
| **`/vehicle-suites/` later** | Same system, functional layer switched on. Re-evaluate only against the trigger above. |
| **Act 03's four states** | Photography and masks. Not geometry. |

**Judgment call, flagged as the most consequential departure in this pass:** the brief
proposed 3D as *potentially primary* for this scene, and the answer is that it should
not be present. The upgrade path is real and cheap — the geometry is authored once and
a renderer can consume the same source — so this is a deferral of cost, not a closed
door.

## 15. The Plan Rail payoff

The rail's whole value is that the visitor does not understand it at first.

| Act | What the visitor sees | What they think |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | One hairline along the bottom of the photograph | Nothing. It reads as part of the building. |
| 01 | It gains a second line, a perpendicular, then two more | *That is a drawing of something.* |
| 02 | It opens out into the whole compound | **"That line has been this place the whole time."** |
| 03 | It returns as a small footprint notation beside the spec plate | *That is the room I am standing in.* |
| 05 | The clubhouse mass illuminates | *I am in that building now.* |
| 07 | It collapses back to the single hairline of act 00 — with one bay still lit | *I picked one.* |

**Understated, and the restraint is the rule.** The recognition at act 02 gets no
announcement, no label reading *THE COMPOUND*, no flourish. It is the drawing simply
being large enough to read, and the visitor doing the rest. A reveal that explains
itself is not a reveal.

**Personalisation is capped at three traces**, deliberately: the footprint notation at
03, the type-and-size line at 07, and the lit bay in the collapsed rail. That is
enough for the choice to feel remembered and not enough for the page to feel like it
is following the visitor around. Anything beyond it — a name, a saved state, a
returning banner — is a CRM feature wearing an art direction.

**And it has an accessibility floor.** The rail's meaning is never carried by the
drawing alone: the current act and the selected suite are always available as real
text in the record column, so a visitor who cannot see the drawing loses none of the
information it reports (`color I2`, `DNA80`).

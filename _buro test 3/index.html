Every version in this folder.

An index, not a portfolio. Each card opens one page. What a version is and what it was built to settle are taken from the repository’s own record — the README, the file’s own title and description, and the commit that shipped it. Nothing here is invented. Some pages have to be served rather than opened from disk, and say so.

The portfolio, and three variants of it

index1 – index4
  • no preview yet 01index1.html

    Selected Work

    The portfolio itself. Six chapters — Cauley Ferrari, Prestige Imports, Pagani of Chicago, Dimamid, MODA Miami for Sotheby’s, Porsche Restoration — on a gallery-neutral ground with no chromatic accent at all.

    Built to settleWhether each case can be shown in its client’s own art direction with the portfolio never putting a frame around it: the only furniture is a rail that sits in the same place on every case, so the eye stops seeing it and starts comparing the work.

  • no preview yet 02index2.html

    Variant B — motion-led

    Each case is a stage pinned with position:sticky and scrubbed by scroll progress. Not variant A with animation added.

    Built to settleWhether the same six chapters read differently as a sequence you move through rather than a document you read. Motion is the deliverable here, so the register comes from the brief and auction-editorial is not selected.

  • no preview yet 03index3.html

    Variant C — the corridor

    Three.js and GSAP. Six planes on a zig-zag corridor with real perspective and fog; scroll drives the camera through it. Serve this one — over file:// it falls back to the document by design, because WebGL cannot use CORS-tainted local images.

    Built to settleThe fair criticism that A and B changed the mechanism but not the language — both a dark plane, a full-bleed photograph and mono labels. This changes the medium: the cases stop being pictures on a page and become objects in a space you travel through.

  • no preview yet 04index4.html

    Variant D — canon page

    A 3D moment for each case, then that case’s own frames unrolling sideways with text, six times. Vertical scroll drives both and the page itself never scrolls sideways. Also needs serving.

    Built to settleTwo things declared rather than borrowed: the Van de Graaf canon as the actual construction system, and an ambient shader that had to earn its place under the ambient tier — achromatic, low amplitude, no bloom.

Meridian Motor Group — one dealership brief, five answers

index5 – index9
  • no preview yet 05index5.html

    Meridian — the free-form design

    A sport-and-exotic dealer site for someone comparing a six-figure purchase. Eight live listings chosen by measurement rather than by eye. The map is a keyless OpenStreetMap embed and will not render from file://.

    Built to settleA stated dialect yield: build dark first is set aside, because every photograph is a white studio sweep and on a dark ground each car becomes a glaring rectangle in a void. Bone ground — and the off-black / off-white rule outlives the direction.

  • no preview yet 06index6.html

    Meridian on the AAN stack

    The same design landed in the ecosystem AAN developers build in — compile-time Sass, no CDN, Bootstrap 4, jQuery, Owl — delivered as HTML plus custom.scss and its compiled CSS, because dev handoff is the point.

    Built to settleWhat index5 was doing that would not have survived handoff: runtime custom properties for theming, a Google Fonts link, and an inventory built from a JS template that left the page empty with scripting off.

  • no preview yet 07index7.html

    The Folio

    A vehicle as an entry in one continuous register: a folio number, a chassis as its real name, and numbered inspection points that bind the photograph to the specification in both directions.

    Built to settleThe criticism of index5, which is not defended: it declared auction-editorial and delivered a dealership template underneath — split hero, search bar, three-column card grid. Bone paint on a stock skeleton. This rebuilds the compositional system.

  • no preview yet 08index8.html

    Night Drive — direction A

    The folio, register and archive concept abandoned entirely. Graphite ground, one warm light on actions only, cinematic cropping, two-up inventory, and one signature interaction: the pass, where a still photograph drifts inside its fixed frame and reads as a car going by.

    Built to settleThe photography problem. All eight sources are bright studio shots, and a dark direction on them risks faking atmosphere. Cutting the cars off their sweeps failed; the answer was to crop tight enough that the car fills the frame.

  • no preview yet 09index9.html

    Night Drive, through the pipeline

    The same brief rebuilt as process rather than surface: the vault queried before any visual decision, a composition pass before styling, three type voices, and a working finance calculator as a real dominant.

    Built to settleFour corrections to index8, each conceded. Cards restored, because banning them threw away what the buyer needs. The palette re-derived from a hue census — 89.4% of every saturated pixel is red, so red is the one hue an action must never take.

Single-surface studies, free topic

index10 – index15
  • no preview yet 10index10.html

    440 — one object, two authorities

    A tuning fork described at once by metrology and by a collector’s register: two documentary systems that both claim authority and are both right. Neither is softened.

    Built to settleWhere two systems can meet. The only fact they share is the number, so the number is the seam — one figure set once, standing in both systems at the same time and clipped exactly on the rule between them.

  • no preview yet 11index11.html

    Rests — the silence at true size

    The six standard rests of 4/4 cut as voids into an ink field, at true relative duration, on one surface. Two values only, so grayscale is the design and there is no accent at all.

    Built to settleWhether figure-ground can be inverted on purpose and stay legible, with dominance measured rather than chosen — the whole rest dominates because it is 50.794% of the bar. Below 48rem the carrier changes from width to height.

  • no preview yet 12index12.html

    Bath — what the dye pot will give you

    A natural-dye predictor. Five dyestuffs, four mordants and four fibres, and every one of the eighty results is computed from a stated model rather than picked by hand.

    Built to settleColour as the function rather than the decoration: the result floods the page because the result is the answer, and the ink is not a token — it is solved against the computed field until it clears 4.5:1, and prints the ratio it reached.

  • no preview yet 13index13.html

    The Second Sunrise

    A fictional 1984 advertising poster for the Macintosh. Drawn geometry in SVG for the machine, CSS for sky, corona, rays, grid and bloom. Not affiliated with Apple.

    Built to settleWhether the promise can be made visible instead of stated. The claim is that the machine is a source of daylight, so the scene is lit by it — remove the Macintosh and the poster goes dark, which is the argument.

  • no preview yet 14index14.html

    Overview — we didn’t invent this colour

    A fictional campaign for an architectural paint brand whose entire range is sampled from orbit. Every frame is NASA, public domain, credited on the sheet; no illustration substitutes for any of it.

    Built to settleThat the reveal belongs in the crop rather than in the copy — the photograph is cropped past the point where it reads as geography and starts reading as a painted surface. The headline is set across a real edge in the picture: one ink, two fields, both legible.

  • no preview yet 15index15.html

    Fovea — the detail was never the problem

    An advertisement for an optics brand whose hero plate is neither a photograph nor a generation: a printed page imaged through two loupes by tracing 224 million photons through a Seidel aberration model of each element.

    Built to settleWhether the product claim can be the artwork. A moulded singlet gives an RMS spot of 27.8px; the aspheric achromat, given the larger aperture, gives 2.2px. The word under the second lens is the brand name, legible through one lens and not the other.

Client and brand studies

index16 · index20 · 360 · miller
  • no preview yet 16index16.html

    Bentley Washington DC

    A retailer page — model range, approved pre-owned, Mulliner commissioning, service and ownership. The first page produced through the brief compiler rather than by reading the taste system by hand. A design study, not affiliated with Bentley Motors.

    Built to settleWhat happens when the palette is given rather than derived. Under an OEM identity standard the green, the whites and the face are mandated, and deriving one here would be a rebrand nobody asked for — so the measurement moved to where the photography sits instead.

  • no preview yet 20index20.html

    Visitor Nexus

    A hero built from three separated plates — sky, mountain, foreground — with pointer parallax as a restrained camera, a route drawn on scroll with three anchored annotations, and a paired transition out of the pin. A design study, not affiliated with Visitor Nexus.

    Built to settleThat each annotation should turn open as one thin cartographic plane hinged on the edge its leader reaches. The previous entrance left roughly 0.13% of the width on screen and read as a plain text fade.

  • no preview yet 360360/index.html

    360 Auto Care, Lynbrook NY

    A complete redesign of the repair-shop preview: diagnostics, maintenance, NYS inspections, tires and major repair. Two drawings constructed by arithmetic rather than traced, and a map built from real OpenStreetMap tiles baked in at build time, so the page still makes no external request.

    Built to settleThe palette by census rather than by choice. 68.4% of all saturation in the nine photographs sits in 10–49°, and the original site’s electric-blue accent sits inside the second band — so it cannot be told apart from the evidence.

  • no preview yet Mmiller/index.html

    Miller Motorcars, Greenwich

    A homepage redesign study for a fifty-year independent house. Names are navigation, logos are evidence, vehicles are the experience — and in the marque field exactly one logo is visible at any moment.

    Built to settleThe changing register as a signature that can be measured: selecting a marque rewrites six compositional tokens — crop ratio, focal position, pace, tracking, air and scale. It moves composition; hue never changes.

## 1. Design Read

- **Deliverable / audience / family** — a single-subject machine study, one page,
  for people who judge an engineering studio by how precisely it presents
  engineering. Family: **instrument-technical** — the page behaves like the
  machine's own documentation rather than like a campaign for it.
- **Mandate** — **REBRAND** for the page's own identity: Buro Lab has no page yet
  and nothing carries through, so invention is expected. **KUKA's product
  identity is explicitly out of scope** — the KR700PA is depicted as it is and is
  not restyled, recoloured into a house palette, or renamed. Depicting a real
  product accurately is the honest position; dressing it in someone else's brand
  would not be.
- **Dialect** — **brief-derived / no stored dialect.** `auction-editorial` is not
  selected and is not the fallback here: an absent brief is not a compatible
  brief, and this subject asks for mechanical legibility and measured density
  rather than air-first restraint and status signalling. What is derived from the
  subject: a dark ground because the machine is read by its highlights, mono
  metadata because every number here is a measurement, and a patient camera
  because the machine itself is unhurried.
- **Dimensionality role** — **MAIN.** The argument is a motion the page cannot
  make in a still. `dimensionality`'s yields-when fires exactly: *the brief is
  genuinely about a made thing whose form is the argument — a physical mechanism
  — and showing it in space communicates faster than describing it.* `DM1`–`DM10`
  therefore bind at full weight, and `DM2` and `DNA74` are not relaxed by MAIN:
  the first headline is HTML text and nothing waits on the canvas.

**Vault:** 40 entries, 20 relevant, 0 unusable for missing notes.
Leaned on `vault/morningstar-ventures` (3, in) — it solves *one sculptural object
must carry a whole first screen without crowding it* by ranking a single dark mass
against a quiet chapter rail, which here means the machine sits small in a large
dark field and the chapter marks are a thin measured column, not a nav. Its
recorded weakness is the one to avoid: *"the page can stop on long transitional
frames where the outgoing text is heavily blurred and the incoming content is
still faint"* — so no cross-blur between chapters, and every stop is legible.
`vault/lapz-io` (3, out) is the anti-reference: *"do not let a powerful
demonstration substitute for information architecture."*

## 2. The concept

**A machine whose arm sweeps three metres and whose hand never tilts — and the
page exists to make you see the not-tilting.**

## 3. The feeling curve (`DNA29`)

| # | Feeling | Caused by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confrontation — *this is bigger than the frame* | The machine crops out of the viewport on all four edges. Its own name sits behind it, dimmed, at 18rem. |
| 2 | Recalibration — *oh, that was one joint* | The frame opens to sixteen metres in a third of a screen. The whole machine, small, in the largest field on the page. |
| 3 | Comprehension — *that is how it holds level* | The pose changes through its range while a hairline stays exactly on the tool plate. |
| 4 | Absorption — *look at the thing itself* | One joint, a metre away. No whole machine, no context, only surface and light. |
| 5 | Resolution | Full extension across the full width, the light resolved, the claim stated plainly. |

No two adjacent shots share a feeling, and the two that move the machine (3 and 5)
are separated by one that does not.

## 4. The peak (`DNA28`)

> *"The arm drives through its whole range and the hand stays dead level — and
> they put a line on screen so you can't unsee it."*

**Shot 03.** It gets the longest extent on the page (1.7 of 6.6), the only lateral
camera travel, and the first appearance of the level line. Shot 04 exists to give
it silence afterwards rather than to add anything.

## 5. Page grammar (`DNA36`)

**Continuous world.** One unbroken scene from first screen to last: one ground,
one light, one camera, no cuts. Chapters are camera positions and typographic
entrances, never new sections with new grounds.

**How it differs from the last build:** the first cut of this same page was a
chaptered editorial wearing a continuous world's clothes — one skeleton, text
left and machine right, five times over. This cut keeps the unbroken scene and
changes what the scene DOES: the crop, the focal length, the pose state and the
light all change per shot, and the type is placed somewhere different in each.
One world, five compositions.

## 6. The signature move (`DNA37`)

**The level line.**

A single hairline rule, drawn **in the DOM and not in the canvas**, locked every
frame to the projected screen height of the tool flange. It spans the full
viewport width. As the arm sweeps, every link in the machine visibly rotates
against it — and the line does not move by one pixel, because the flange does not.

It is not a parameter change to a known device. The mechanism is that a page
element is driven by a projected 3D point and is then *proven not to move*: the
device's entire content is its own stillness. Described to someone who has seen
other scroll builds, it is not a reveal, a pin, a scrub or a parallax — it is a
measuring instrument laid over a machine, and it is doing the one job the page
exists to do (`C13` — the device serves the subject and never takes its place).

**Its removal test (`MJ10`):** take the line out and the flange still stays level,
but nobody notices, because a thing that does not move is invisible without a
reference. What disappears with the device is the *comprehension*, not the
spectacle. That is the test passing.

## 7. Shot list (`DNA27`, `DNA50`)

**Rebuilt 2026-09-02, second cut.** The first cut interpolated between five
similar three-quarter views: text left, machine right, five times. It was
technically a shot list and compositionally one shot shown five ways. This cut is
built from opposites, and no two adjacent shots share a distance, a focal length,
a pose state or a light.

| # | Shot | Frame | Pose | Light | vh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Impact | **macro/crop** — 2.4 m out, 44° | the machine overflows the frame; type BEHIND it | A, held | sculptural rim, almost no fill | 1.2 |
| 02 Scale | **release** — 16 m out, 28° | whole machine, small, largest negative space on the page | A, held | clean and even | 1.1 |
| 03 Articulation | **dolly** — lateral, 5.9 m, 34° | the pose changes; camera crosses | **A → C** | hard side light, rods shadow the castings | 1.7 |
| 04 Macro | **macro** — 1.2 m out, 26° | one joint, nothing else in frame | C, held | specular, tight and hot | 1.1 |
| 05 Release | **release** — 7.4 m out, 38° | full extension across the full width, type crossed by the arm | **C → D** | resolved hero | 1.5 |

**Total: 6.6 viewport-heights** (was 9.0). Plus the record.

**The choreography is deliberately out of phase.** In 03 the camera starts first
and the arm finishes before it; in 05 the arm reaches full extension at 70% while
the frame is still opening. Camera and machine starting and stopping together
reads as one mechanism rather than a machine being filmed.

**Stillness → mechanical action → stillness.** Only two shots move the machine.
The other three hold it, which is what makes 03 and 05 read as events.

## 8. Mobile shot list (`DNA67`, `MJ8`)

**Authored, not derived.** A portrait frame has roughly half the horizontal field,
so every shot carries its own distance and focal length in `story.js` — the
desktop crop at 390px eats the machine entirely. Five shots are kept; what changes
is that every camera pulls back and widens (44°→50°, 34°→42°), the type moves to
the bottom of the frame in every shot behind a full-height gradient, and the
masthead drops difference-blending because it sits over lit orange for most of the
scroll rather than over the dark hall.

**Total: 8.0 viewport-heights at 390×844.**

## 12. Reference (`DNA3`)

No storyboard or reference image exists. The primary visual authority is therefore
**the machine's own geometry**, which is unusually strong evidence: the survey
table in `assets/robo hand/step-survey.json` gives every mass, extent and centroid,
and the composition is built against those rather than against a mood.

Where a judgement had no geometric answer — exposure, the ground's fall-off, the
type scale — it is recorded under *Judgment calls* in the delivery report.
